01 Thèse de doctorat

Fractional Order Model for Evolution of Bovine Tuberculosis and Rabies, and their Co-infection with Optimal Control and Cost-Effectiveness — PAUSTI / JKUAT, Nairobi, Kenya, 2024.

This thesis develops three fractional-order compartmental models for bovine tuberculosis (bTB), rabies, and their co-infection — zoonoses caused by Mycobacterium bovis and lyssavirus. Basic reproduction numbers are derived via the next-generation matrix method, and sensitivity analysis identifies key transmission drivers: dairy-product consumption for bTB, and dog vaccination and transmission rates for rabies. Disease-free equilibria are shown globally asymptotically stable when reproduction numbers are below one. The co-infection model is extended into an optimal control problem, solved via the forward-backward sweep method, showing that combining animal-focused prevention, removal of rabies-affected animals, and joint bTB-rabies vaccination is most effective. Models are validated numerically in MATLAB using the fde12 algorithm. Findings support One Health policy and public health authorities in designing evidence-based, cost-effective zoonotic disease control strategies.
